TURN 2 — Assistant's first reaction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What's solid:
  - Orientation/gravity diagnosis correct. Different mean gravity = different
    physical placement.
  - Magnitude as a rotation-invariant feature is a legit standard HAR fix.
  - PSI on outputs is reasonable as one unsupervised signal.
  - mg → m/s² conversion correct (verify per source).

Pushback:
  1. "Magnitude completely solves" — overstated. Direction is signal for
     hand-tapping vs ear-rubbing; magnitude collapses all directional info.
  2. "Drop all 6 raw channels" — too aggressive. Better: gravity-aligned
     rotation, OR augment with magnitudes (8 channels), not replace.
  3. "9.82 ≈ 9.86 means distributions match" — misleading. Mean magnitude is
     ≈ g during quasi-static periods by definition. The gyro 46 → 60.5 is the
     real signal and magnitude does NOT fix that.
  4. "Drop original axes" — sledgehammer. Gravity-aligned rotation preserves
     direction while removing orientation drift.
  5. PSI on outputs is a tripwire, not a correctness check. A model can
     preserve class proportions while being systematically wrong.

Bottom line: Use the diagnosis, not the prescription. The prescription
optimizes for "alarms stop" rather than "model gets robust."

TURN 6 — Assistant's pushback (sharper this round)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Math is right. Diagnosis is right. Prescription is half-right.

Confirmed correct:
  - Post-z arithmetic verified — alerts will fire as Gemini calculated.
  - "Scaler can't rotate axes" is correct and important.
  - Existing DomainCalibrator (per-channel mean shift) is a 1D band-aid for
    the same reason.

Pushback:
  1. Drop-all-six is too aggressive. Hand-tapping has a directional axis;
     ear-rubbing is lateral oscillation; walking has a vertical component.
     Magnitude collapses all of these. The hand-tapping collapse debugging
     script already exists — magnitude-only would likely make it worse.

  2. Better intervention is gravity-aligned reframing:
       (a) Per-window gravity rotation — keep all 6 channels.
       (b) Augment, don't replace — 6 axes + 2 magnitudes (8 channels).
       (c) High-pass gravity removal — fixes mean only, not variance.
       (d) Magnitude-only (what Gemini wants) — biggest information loss.

  3. "Relax σ to 1.8 for G_mag" — this is hiding the alarm. The 1.7σ gyro
     spread is real behavioral drift. The proper fix is broader training
     data, not threshold relaxation.

  4. "PSI well over 1.0" — plausible but verify with the actual drift monitor
     against the on-disk production windows.

Plan:
  Step 1: Run gravity-aligned rotation as a read-only experiment.
  Step 2: Only replace features after measurement, not before.
  Step 3: Don't relax the gyro σ threshold.

Key findings:
  ✓ Az std drift fully resolved (2.20 → 1.19).
  ✓ Ax/Ay mean alerts cleared.
  ✗ Gy/Gz std got WORSE (rotation mixes high-variance Gx into Gy/Gz).
    → Conclusively proves gyro variance drift is real motion, not orientation.

  ✗ Az mean alert remains (-0.73). Cause: training "gravity vector" is

vs phone on desk).

Recommendation: re-run with (a) low-pass-estimated gravity per session aligned
to canonical [0, 0, -9.81], and (b) per-window rotation instead of per-session.
